r<=norm(z0-w), norm(z-z0)<r/2. They bound the actual normalized complex log by
one and prove its actual complex derivative (z-w)^(-1). Positive source
separation and membership in the principal logarithm's slit plane are proved.
No global branch assertion is made.

The normalized-integrability and differentiated-integral theorems use any
source subtype S, arbitrary mu, and real Integrable f. Uniform separation is
an explicit hypothesis for these local interfaces. The local disk is open
ball(z0,r/2). Absolute density integrability dominates the local log by |f|
and its derivative by |f|/(r/2). Every differentiation-under-integral
hypothesis, including measurability and neighborhood domination, is supplied.
No finite-measure assumption or extra compactness is present at this stage.

The outside-source integrability theorem takes compact S, arbitrary mu on S,
real integrable f, and z outside S. Compactness bounds the continuous real
logarithmic kernel on S; the actual integrand is absolutely integrable.
The final two theorems prove HarmonicAt the actual real source integral at
every z0 outside S and HarmonicOnNhd that same function on the full complement.
Compactness supplies positive local separation. The potential is identified
locally with its value at z0 plus the real part of the normalized holomorphic
integral. The real-log identity uses proved nonzero denominators at z and z0.
This covers multiply connected exteriors and irregular compact boundaries.

Source values or source-point harmonicity are not asserted. Integrable f,
rather than finiteness of mu, supplies finite absolute density and is a valid
strengthening of the finite-measure planned interface. These are analytic
supporting theorems; no capacity, equilibrium, support-hull, or final Erdős
conclusion is assumed or proved. All dependencies have only propext,
Classical.choice, Quot.sound. No correspondence discrepancy found.
